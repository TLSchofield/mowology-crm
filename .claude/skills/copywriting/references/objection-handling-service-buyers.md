# Objection Handling for Service Buyers

A working map for pre-empting and answering objections in copy that sells a recurring local service (grounds maintenance, cleaning, snow, trades) to three buyers: strata councils, property managers, and homeowners. Built from sources already in this skill; each technique cites where it comes from so you can go deeper.

Use it when writing a service landing page, a quote follow-up, a proposal cover letter, a FAQ block, or a guide that has to move a council from "we have someone" to "let's get a quote."

---

## 1. The principle underneath all of it

- **Sequence the copy by the buyer's questions, in the order a live buyer would ask them.** Each section raises the objection and resolves it before the reader has to voice it. Sugarman calls this the slippery slide; a reader who hits an unanswered question stops sliding. (`sugarman-handbook.md`, the raise-and-resolve pass.)
- **Enter the conversation already in the prospect's head.** The objection they haven't typed into the contact form is the one that decides. Collier's rule: you don't create the doubt, you name the one they already hold and answer it. (`legends-library.md`, Collier.)
- **Inoculate.** Name the competitor's future counter-argument first and refute it, so that when the cheaper quote arrives the council already has the frame for reading it. (`whitman-cashvertising.md`, Inoculation Theory.)
- **Specifics beat reassurance.** "Fully insured" is a claim; "$5M liability, WorkSafeBC clearance letter on request" is an answer. (`hopkins`-era rule via `caples-tested-advertising-methods.md`; Ogilvy's "facts sell.")
- **A damaging admission buys credibility for everything after it.** Saying what you don't do, or where you're not the cheapest, makes the next claim believable. (`kennedy-ultimate-sales-letter.md`, the damaging-admission technique.)
- **Price objections are reframed, not argued.** Kennedy's five reframes: break it down per unit of use, compare to the cost of the problem, compare to the alternative's true cost, anchor against a bigger number, show the delay cost. (`kennedy-ultimate-sales-letter.md`.)

Never invent a statistic or a testimonial to answer an objection. If the honest answer is "it depends on the site," write that, then say how it gets settled (a site walk). See `legal-compliance-guardrails.md` before quoting any number or guarantee.

## 2. The five objection families

Every objection a service buyer raises falls into one of five families. Diagnose the family first; the answer pattern follows.

| Family | What it sounds like | Answer pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Money** | "Your quote is higher." "Can we get it cheaper?" | Reframe (per visit, per unit, per year), compare against the real alternative's full cost, show what the cheaper quote leaves out. |
| **Trust** | "How do we know the work gets done?" "We've been burned before." | Proof stack (§4), named process, records they can inspect, a specific past failure you fixed. |
| **Fit** | "Our property is unusual." "Do you do X as well?" | Name the property types you serve with specifics; be explicit about what's out of scope. A clean "no" on one item makes the "yes" on the rest credible. |
| **Timing / urgency** | "Let's revisit at the AGM." "Not until spring." | Cost of delay stated plainly (a missed fall cleanup is a spring problem; a winter without a plan is a liability); an easy first step that doesn't need a vote. |
| **Authority / process** | "I have to take it to council." "We need three quotes." | Give them the artefact that wins the room: a one-page scope with month-by-month calendar, a comparison sheet, references they can phone. Make the internal sale easy. |

## 3. Objections by buyer, with the honest answer

### Strata council (volunteers, risk-averse, decide by committee)

| Objection | What's really behind it | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| "We already have a contractor." | Switching cost, fear of a worse outcome | Don't attack the incumbent. Offer a no-obligation site walk and a written scope they can hold the incumbent to. If the incumbent is fine, say so; you've earned the next call. |
| "Your quote is higher." | Comparing numbers that describe different jobs | Put the scopes side by side. Ask what the cheaper quote includes for fall leaf passes, hedge trims, green-waste disposal, drain clearing. Reframe as monthly per unit. |
| "How do we know you showed up?" | Past experience of invisible work | Photo-verified visits after every service, sent to a named contact. Show a real report from another property (with permission). |
| "We need three quotes." | Bylaw or habit, not resistance | Agree, and hand them the RFP checklist so the three quotes are comparable. You become the one that helped them compare. |
| "Council can't decide until the AGM." | No one wants to own the decision | Offer a start date that follows the meeting and a spring cleanup as the first job so the new contract begins on a clean property. Name the cost of a missed season. |
| "Can you handle snow as well?" | Liability lives in winter | Yes, with a written priority map, salting triggers, and a time-stamped log of every visit. Say clearly it's for strata and commercial, not single homes, if that's the truth. |
| "What if a resident complains?" | Councils absorb complaints | Named account contact, response time, and a process. A story of one complaint handled well beats a paragraph of promises. |

### Property manager (paid to reduce their own workload and exposure)

| Objection | Behind it | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| "I need one number for the year." | Budget cycles, no surprises | Level monthly fee with seasonal services named inside it; extras priced in advance. |
| "I manage twelve buildings; I can't chase you." | Communication load | One contact, photo reports without asking, a monthly summary. State the response time. |
| "Who's liable if someone slips?" | Their name is on it | Insurance limit, WorkSafeBC clearance, the winter visit log, and the municipal bylaw deadline you work to. |
| "Will it be the same crew?" | Continuity | Dedicated crew per route, and what happens when someone is away. |

### Homeowner (personal money, personal garden)

| Objection | Behind it | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| "I could do it myself." | Cost vs. Saturday | Don't argue. Price it per visit and let them weigh the Saturday. Offer the jobs they can't easily do themselves (hedges, aeration, cleanups). |
| "Is it worth it for a small yard?" | Fear of overpaying | Say what a small yard costs per visit if you can, or say you price after seeing it and quote the same day. |
| "Are you insured? Who's coming?" | Strangers on the property | Insurance, named crew, photo when done, no need to be home. |
| "It's just a one-off." | Don't want a contract | Accept one-offs where true. The cleanup that goes well is how the weekly plan starts. |

## 4. Proof: what answers a trust objection, in order of strength

1. **Records the buyer can inspect** (visit logs, photo reports, a sample winter log).
2. **A named, reachable reference** at a comparable property.
3. **A specific case** with a number that is true and verifiable (before/after, dates, what changed).
4. **Third-party ratings** the buyer can check themselves (Google, HomeStars, BBB), with the count.
5. **Credentials** (insurance limit, WorkSafeBC, licences, associations).
6. **Testimonial quotes.** Weakest alone; strong as colour on top of 1–5.

Place proof immediately after the objection it answers, not in a separate "testimonials" block the reader never reaches. (Placement rule from `sugarman-handbook.md`; proof ranking adapted from `bly-copywriters-handbook.md` and `kennedy-ultimate-sales-letter.md`.)

## 5. Objections change with awareness stage

Schwartz's stages (`schwartz-breakthrough-advertising.md`) decide which objection is live:

| Stage | Live objection | What the copy must do first |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware ("the grounds are fine") | None yet; no pain felt | Show the cost of the current state (a drain clogged in November, a slip claim) before selling anything. |
| Problem-aware ("the property looks tired") | "Is it the contractor or the budget?" | Diagnose. Name the three things a tired property usually has in common. |
| Solution-aware ("we should tender") | "How do we compare quotes?" | Give them the comparison tool. The RFP checklist is the whole answer. |
| Product-aware ("we're considering Mowology") | Money, trust, fit | The §3 table. Proof placed next to each. |
| Most aware ("send the proposal") | Process and timing | Remove steps: site walk booked in one message, proposal in X days, start date. |

Writing a product-aware answer for a problem-aware reader is the most common failure: it sounds like pressure because it answers a question they haven't reached.

## 6. Where objections go on the page and in the sequence

**Service landing page**
1. Quick answer (40–60 words) that names the buyer and the outcome.
2. Checklist of what's included: this pre-empts "what does it cover."
3. "Why councils choose us": one card per objection family, proof inside each.
4. How it works: three steps, each removing a process objection.
5. FAQ: the objections you couldn't fit above, as literal questions in the buyer's words. Keep each answer self-contained and 40–60 words; this is also what AI answer engines extract.
6. CTA with a risk reversal: free site walk, no obligation, proposal in writing.

**Quote follow-up email (day 3)**
Lead with the one objection you heard on the site walk, answer it in two sentences, attach the artefact that helps them decide (scope sheet, comparison, reference). Never "just checking in." See `email-sequence` for cadence.

**Proposal cover letter**
Open with their words for their problem (from the site walk), then the three objections you expect the council to raise and the answer to each, then the price with its reframe. The council member forwards this; write it so it survives forwarding.

## 7. Techniques, quick reference

- **Raise and resolve** in the same breath. Don't leave the objection standing across a section break.
- **Acknowledge before answering.** "You're right to ask what the cheaper quote leaves out" costs nothing and lowers the guard.
- **Reframe the unit.** Monthly per unit for strata; per visit for homeowners; per year for property managers.
- **Compare against the real alternative,** not against competitors by name: in-house caretaker, the cheapest quote's true scope, doing nothing until spring.
- **Damaging admission.** "We're rarely the cheapest quote on the table" followed by why.
- **Show the process, not the promise.** A priority map beats "we respond fast."
- **Turn the objection into a criterion.** "Ask every bidder how they document a visit" makes your strength the buyer's checklist.
- **Story over assertion** for trust objections: one specific incident handled well.
- **Name the out-of-scope.** Saying no to single-home snow removal makes the strata offer credible.
- **Give the internal seller ammunition.** For committee buyers, the artefact matters more than the argument.
- **Cost of delay, stated once, without pressure.** The season is the deadline, not your offer.
- **Guarantee what you control.** A satisfaction re-do on a missed area is credible; "guaranteed results" on living plants is not, and is a compliance flag.

## 8. Pre-ship check

- [ ] Every objection in §3 that applies to this buyer is answered somewhere on the page, in their words.
- [ ] Each answer sits next to its proof, and the proof is true and verifiable.
- [ ] At least one damaging admission or explicit out-of-scope statement.
- [ ] Price objection is reframed, not defended.
- [ ] The awareness stage of the traffic matches the objections answered first.
- [ ] FAQ answers are self-contained, 40–60 words, no marketing filler.
- [ ] No invented numbers, no named-competitor claims, no guarantee you can't honour (`legal-compliance-guardrails.md` §11).
