Positioning line to reason from (not a tagline): *the landscaping contractor whose every visit is documented.*

## 3. Who we write to

- **Strata council member.** Volunteer, risk-averse, decides by committee, compares three quotes that describe different jobs, absorbs resident complaints. Wants: no surprises, a record, someone who shows up.
- **Property manager.** Paid to reduce their own workload and exposure across many buildings. Wants: one number for the year, one contact, no chasing, liability covered.
- **Homeowner.** Personal money, personal garden, values friendliness and being listened to ("they do exactly what I ask"). Wants: reliable weekly service, a crew they recognise, and photos when they're not home.

Awareness on arrival: search traffic for a service is solution- or product-aware; a cold council is problem-aware at best. Pick the lead accordingly (`copywriting/references/masterson-forde-great-leads.md`).

## 6. Brand voice: seven-axis analysis

Scored from the owner's own writing. Evidence lines are quoted.

| Axis | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm | Short to medium, declarative, one idea per sentence; comfortable with a fragment for effect. | "no fumes in your garden, less noise in the neighbourhood, and a clean cut every time." (Instagram bio) / "CrossFit until the shoulder went." (talkinghands.ca) |
| Formality | Plain-spoken, contractions always, first names ("Tim and company" is how customers refer to it). Reads at a kitchen table, not a boardroom. | "some large some small but all with a personal yet professional service" (directory blurb) |
| Self-reference | "We" and "our crews"; the owner is a person customers name, but the copy speaks for the crew, not for Tim. First person "I" only in signed emails and review replies. | "Our crews take care of properties throughout Vancouver" |
| Humour and warmth | Warm, understated, occasionally dry. Not jokey. Warmth comes from noticing the customer's situation, not from exclamation marks. | "You've probably already tried the thing that works until it doesn't." |
| Directness | Flat statements, including about limits and scope. Says what it is and isn't. | "If you want to spar from the first month ... this is not the school for you." / "We never cut corners." / "rain or shine" |
| Vocabulary | Concrete, physical, everyday: cut, crew, visit, photo, property, show up. One distinctive move: naming the benefit in the neighbour's terms (less noise in the neighbourhood). Mild fondness for "a higher degree of service" and the "science of landscaping" idea behind the name. | "there's a science to great landscaping" (About) |
| Never | No exclamation points in marketing copy; no "solutions", "leverage", "exceptional", "elevate"; no emoji in body copy; no superlatives without a number behind them; no claims about design or artistry. | Evidence by absence across the owner-written samples. The exclamation marks in the CRM email templates and the "Ready to Elevate Your Property's Landscape?" homepage line are the counter-examples, and both were written for the owner, not by him. |

### Conflict flagged
The site and the email templates are chirpier than the owner is: "Thank you for reaching out!", "sometimes these land in junk mail!", "Ready to Elevate…". The owner's own writing is calmer and plainer. **Recommendation:** new copy follows the card below; retune the quote and invoice email templates in the CRM to match (drop the exclamation marks, keep the warmth) when they're next touched.

## 7. Brand voice card

```
BRAND VOICE CARD — Mowology

Sentence rhythm: short, declarative; one idea per sentence; a fragment is fine for punch
Formality: plain-spoken, contractions always; kitchen table, not boardroom
Self-reference: "we" / "our crew"; "I" only in signed emails and review replies (Tim)
Humour: warm and understated; the warmth is in noticing the customer's situation
Directness: say what it is, what it isn't, and what it costs to wait; no hedging
Vocabulary: concrete and physical (cut, crew, visit, photo, show up); the neighbour's-eye benefit
Never: exclamation points, "solution/leverage/elevate/exceptional", emoji in body copy,
       superlatives without a number, claims about design flair
```

### Voice test
Sample sentence (owner): "no fumes in your garden, less noise in the neighbourhood, and a clean cut every time."
New sentence on another topic, same card: "Photos after every visit, a crew that knows your property, and no chasing anyone for an answer."
Same person could have written both: rhythm of three, concrete nouns, no adjective doing the work.

Violation test: "We're thrilled to elevate your outdoor space with exceptional service!" versus "We cut it, we clear up, we send you the photo." The second is on brand; the first is what the card exists to stop.

## 8. Channel notes

- **Website:** service pages open with a direct answer for solution-aware searchers; proof next to each claim; FAQ answers 40–60 words for AI extraction.
- **Quote follow-up email (day 3):** lead with the one objection heard on the site walk; attach the scope sheet; sign as Tim. See `copywriting/references/objection-handling-service-buyers.md`.
- **Proposal cover letter:** written to survive being forwarded around a council; three expected objections answered; price reframed per unit per month.
- **Social:** a before/after and one specific sentence in the customer's terms. No hashtag walls.
- **Trucks, shirts, signage:** one line, thirty feet, three seconds. Candidates to test: "Photo-verified landscaping." / "We send you the photo." Keep the phone number.
- **SMS:** already constrained by carrier rules (160 chars, no URLs, phone number included); the voice card still applies to the words.
