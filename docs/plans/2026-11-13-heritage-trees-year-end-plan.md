## Findings that shape the plan
1. **A locked TD PDF fails on the real upload page** (reproduced locally 2026-11-13): "Secured pdf file are currently not supported" (HTTP 500). The file uses the standard security handler (RC4-128, empty user password): it opens for the owner, copying/printing is restricted. `previewPdf()` calls `parseFile()` outside any try/catch, and the OCR fallback runs only on garbled text. Production blocks `exec`/`proc_open` for crons (reference_prod_cron_php81), so fixing it with `qpdf`/`pdftotext` is not safe to assume.
2. **Penny's receipt prompt assumes a landscaper.** For an arborist (chainsaws, chipper rental, climbing gear, disposal fees, a bucket truck) the vehicle/equipment rules and example text are wrong. Not dumbing Penny down means this is a prerequisite, not polish.
3. **"Unlocked heads" is the same mechanism Claudia needs.** Build it once, for both, as the existing plan already specifies; Sam's setting is `penny` fully on (not snap-only).
4. **The accountant package is mostly an existing login.** `AccountantFence` + the Ledger view is the accountant's side; what is unknown is whether the Ledger view offers a trial balance, general ledger and P&L, and how receipts are reached from it.

5. **Sam works in one batch, not as he goes (Sam, 2026-11-13): he leaves it all until the end of the year, then sits down for about two weeks.** So the design target is a backlog session: a full year of statements and a pile of receipts, one person, two weeks. That changes four things: (a) bulk intake (many photos at once from the camera roll, 12 months of statements) matters more than the single snap; (b) the daily AI cap must be sized for hundreds of receipts in a few days, not a trickle; (c) Penny's overnight work (reads, matching, filing) needs the tenant cron runner, which is not built, so batch reads must run on request, with visible progress; (d) duplicates are likely (the same receipt photographed twice), so the duplicate checks and the Merge/Undo flow matter. "Snap straight away" stays as a short trial so he sees Penny work, but nothing depends on him doing it early.
6. **By then he has forgotten what things were for (Sam, 2026-11-13).** So Penny's questions must carry the memory for him: show the receipt photo, vendor, date, line items and the bank line side by side, with her best guess from the arborist profile, and let him answer by tapping ("Chainsaw parts / Fuel / Equipment rental / Disposal / Insurance / Other / Personal") instead of typing. A two-second "what was this for?" at snap time is the real cure, so the short trial teaches that habit even if the bulk still comes in January. Because he is incorporated, anything personal must go to the shareholder account, never be left blank or guessed as a business cost; "I don't know" lands in a visible list the accountant sees, not in an expense. Penny already learns from corrections and keeps a memo of why each entry was booked; his answers feed both.
7. **What "getting it together for the accountant" really is (Tim, 2026-11-13): three piles, not many: receipts, statements, and Sam's own invoices.** The plan so far covers the first two. His **sales invoices** are the income side: what he billed, the GST he collected, and what is still unpaid at year-end. He issues them outside Ittai (spreadsheet, Word or paper: ask in onboarding), so Penny needs a way to take them in (upload or forward a PDF/photo, read it, match the bank deposit to it, flag unpaid ones). **Unknown, to settle by reading:** whether the receipt reader and the invoice-deposit reconciliation already accept an outside invoice as income, or whether that is a new path; the answer sizes Stage 5. Tim's Mail has one email from Sam (Sept 2020, City of Vancouver tree-removal permit forms, no invoices); his past invoices are not in that mailbox.
8. **His calendar as a memory aid (optional, Tim's question 2026-11-13).** With Sam's own permission only (Google sign-in with a read-only calendar scope, or the iPhone's calendar permission read on the phone), a receipt dated on a job day can show "Tue: removal at <address>", which answers "what was this for?" without him remembering. Nothing is read without his tap; nothing is kept beyond the matched title, date and place. Not for the first release: Google calendar scopes need app verification beyond test users, and Apple calendars need the on-phone permission path; decide after the volume test.

## Steps, each proved before the next
**Stage 0 — decisions (this week).** Tim, 2026-11-13: Sam's answers are not collected by Tim by hand; they are **questions in the onboarding screens being built** (`OnboardingQuestions`, which today holds only Mowology/landscaping rules, so a books-only set is added). The phone type needs no question: the app knows what it runs on, and the iPhone invite needs his Apple ID email only at install.

**Stage 6 — the backlog session (replaces "snap first week").** Order for Sam's two weeks: (1) all 12 months of bank and card statements first (CSV preferred; PDF once Stage 1 is live), (2) Penny matches what she can, (3) the list of bank lines with no receipt tells him which receipts to dig out, (4) he photographs them in batches (multi-select from the camera roll; no one-at-a-time screen), (5) he works down Penny's questions queue with a visible count ("212 of 380 done"), clean matches confirmed in bulk. Needs: bulk receipt intake; reads that run on request with progress (no cron dependency); a daily cap sized for the backlog (set from a volume test below); duplicate handling tuned for double photos. Tim sits with him for the first hour. A short trial before then (five receipts, one statement) so nothing is new on the big day.
**Memory aids for a man who forgets (finding 6):** the question screen shows the receipt image, the matched bank line, the vendor's history in his own books ("last four Husqvarna dealer purchases were Tools") and one tap per likely answer; "Personal" books to the shareholder account; "I don't know" goes to an open-items list on the accountant package. Vendor and category answers are remembered, so the second Husqvarna receipt needs no question. A one-tap "what was this for?" row on the snap screen at the time of purchase (optional, off for Mowology) is the habit to teach in the trial.
**One question per receipt (Tim, 2026-11-13: "she currently asks for a lot of receipt information that is missing. there should be just one question").** For Sam, Penny's work is the same, her asking is not: she reads, checks tax and totals, matches the bank line and picks the category herself, writes the reason in the memo, and asks **at most one thing per receipt**. Order of the one question, first that applies: (1) *What was this for?* when the purpose or business-versus-personal is not clear (tap answers, finding 6); (2) *Same purchase or two?* when a real twin exists; (3) otherwise nothing: the receipt files itself with its memo. Rules: never ask for a field she can read or infer (vendor, date, total, tax, payment method); an unreadable receipt gets *"retake this photo"*, never a form of blanks; fields still missing after her best read go to the open-items list for the accountant, not to Sam; bank lines with no receipt are one bulk list ("no receipt needed": bank fees, interest, transfers she can name, versus "find me a receipt"), not a question per line; job and landscaping questions (`unbilled_materials`, service-account and job-billing prompts) are off for a business with no jobs. Implemented as a per-business setting `penny_one_question` (absent = today's behaviour, so Mowology is unchanged), with a test that a receipt with several missing fields yields exactly one question, and measured in the volume test (target: at most 1.0 questions per receipt, and a median of 0 for clean receipts). Unknown, settled by reading `PennyQuestionService`, the receipt review screen and the Penny card carousel: the full list of things she asks for today, so each is mapped to "decide herself", "one question" or "open list".
**Caps that a backlog session hits (verified in code 2026-11-13):** (1) `ReceiptIntakeService` allows **20 receipt uploads per user per hour**, hard-coded (`isRateLimited`): 400 receipts is 20 hours of waiting. Fix: a per-business setting `receipt_upload_hourly_limit`, default 20 so Mowology is unchanged; Sam's raised for the session. (2) Penny prepares **40 receipts a day** by default (`BookkeeperDeskService::DEFAULT_DAILY_CAP`, per-business `ops_settings.bookkeeper_daily_cap`): 400 receipts is 10 days, 600 does not fit in his two weeks. Raise it for Sam's business only, sized by the volume test and by what the reads cost (Opus 5.5 at $4 / $20 per million tokens in/out; the usage log gives cost per receipt). (3) `ReceiptFactsService::REOCR_DAILY_CAP` (40) applies to re-reads of old receipts, not new ones. Unknown: how many receipts Sam has (ask him); whether the upload limit is also per IP or per business elsewhere.
**Volume test (local site, before Sam):** 12 synthetic statements (about 1,500 lines) and 400 synthetic receipt images through the whole flow: time per step, AI cost per receipt, memory and timeouts on shared hosting, what breaks. The result sets the daily cap and shows whether on-request reads can keep up.

**Stage 7 (optional, after year-end if it does not fit) — his own Ittai address.** One shared receiving mailbox on `ittai.ca`, one address per tenant, a router poller that maps address to tenant, per-tenant sender allow-list (Sam + accountant). Statements by email go to a preview step, not the receipt flow. Costed after unknown 7.

## Safeguards
- **Nothing changes for existing businesses.** Every new fence, profile and hook is a no-op without its setting; golden and byte-identical tests prove it; Mowology's prompt and menus are unchanged.
- **His data stays his.** Separate database, separate uploads folder; a cross-tenant test (his receipts and vendors never appear in Mowology, and the reverse). His contribution to the shared vendor knowledge stays OFF until he agrees.
- **No real statements in the repo, tests or commits.** Fixtures are synthetic. Tim's TD file stays in Downloads.
- **Privacy-preserving problem reports.** If a statement will not read, the app offers "send us just the layout" (masked header row and field shapes, no amounts or names) rather than the file. Small new piece, included in Stage 1 if it fits, else Stage 6.
- **Deploys** follow the usual gate: commit, merge to feature/language, phpunit, deploy-safe, render the pages, OPcache reset, verify live.
- **Codex first** for the review of every stage (money, tenancy, auth); fall back to the code-reviewer agent if Codex is out of usage.
