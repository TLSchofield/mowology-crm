## Findings that shape the plan
1. **A locked TD PDF fails on the real upload page** (reproduced locally 2026-11-13): "Secured pdf file are currently not supported" (HTTP 500). The file uses the standard security handler (RC4-128, empty user password): it opens for the owner, copying/printing is restricted. `previewPdf()` calls `parseFile()` outside any try/catch, and the OCR fallback runs only on garbled text. Production blocks `exec`/`proc_open` for crons (reference_prod_cron_php81), so fixing it with `qpdf`/`pdftotext` is not safe to assume.
2. **Penny's receipt prompt assumes a landscaper.** For an arborist (chainsaws, chipper rental, climbing gear, disposal fees, a bucket truck) the vehicle/equipment rules and example text are wrong. Not dumbing Penny down means this is a prerequisite, not polish.
3. **"Unlocked heads" is the same mechanism Claudia needs.** Build it once, for both, as the existing plan already specifies; Sam's setting is `penny` fully on (not snap-only).
4. **The accountant package is mostly an existing login.** `AccountantFence` + the Ledger view is the accountant's side; what is unknown is whether the Ledger view offers a trial balance, general ledger and P&L, and how receipts are reached from it.

5. **Sam works in one batch, not as he goes (Sam, 2026-11-13): he leaves it all until the end of the year, then sits down for about two weeks.** So the design target is a backlog session: a full year of statements and a pile of receipts, one person, two weeks. That changes four things: (a) bulk intake (many photos at once from the camera roll, 12 months of statements) matters more than the single snap; (b) the daily AI cap must be sized for hundreds of receipts in a few days, not a trickle; (c) Penny's overnight work (reads, matching, filing) needs the tenant cron runner, which is not built, so batch reads must run on request, with visible progress; (d) duplicates are likely (the same receipt photographed twice), so the duplicate checks and the Merge/Undo flow matter. "Snap straight away" stays as a short trial so he sees Penny work, but nothing depends on him doing it early.
6. **By then he has forgotten what things were for (Sam, 2026-11-13).** So Penny's questions must carry the memory for him: show the receipt photo, vendor, date, line items and the bank line side by side, with her best guess from the arborist profile, and let him answer by tapping ("Chainsaw parts / Fuel / Equipment rental / Disposal / Insurance / Other / Personal") instead of typing. A two-second "what was this for?" at snap time is the real cure, so the short trial teaches that habit even if the bulk still comes in January. Because he is incorporated, anything personal must go to the shareholder account, never be left blank or guessed as a business cost; "I don't know" lands in a visible list the accountant sees, not in an expense. Penny already learns from corrections and keeps a memo of why each entry was booked; his answers feed both.
7. **What "getting it together for the accountant" really is (Tim, 2026-11-13): three piles, not many: receipts, statements, and Sam's own invoices.** The plan so far covers the first two. His **sales invoices** are the income side: what he billed, the GST he collected, and what is still unpaid at year-end. He issues them outside Ittai (spreadsheet, Word or paper: ask in onboarding), so Penny needs a way to take them in (upload or forward a PDF/photo, read it, match the bank deposit to it, flag unpaid ones). **Unknown, to settle by reading:** whether the receipt reader and the invoice-deposit reconciliation already accept an outside invoice as income, or whether that is a new path; the answer sizes Stage 5.
