# Eight Visual Formats Every Brand Should Test in 2026

*Source: Motion (motionapp.com) — based on analysis of 474,560 Meta ads*

---

## Five Proven Ad Formats

### 1. Testimonial
Real person sharing their experience with genuine reactions — like a friend telling a story.

**Key:** Social proof language — problem → solution tried → result with your product. Use customer reviews for authentic language.

**Best for:** Building trust with skeptical audiences, products with strong emotional outcomes, high-consideration purchases.

**Choose when:**
- Product solves a problem people have tried and failed to fix before
- Outcome is transformative and hard to believe without proof
- Audience needs social permission to buy ("someone like me" succeeded)
- Crowded category where claims all sound the same

**Audience fit:** Solution-aware and product-aware audiences who need reassurance. Cold/unaware audiences won't respond.

**Budget:** Low to mid. Authentic UGC often outperforms high-production versions.

---

### 2. Demo
Demonstrating what your product does, how it works, or the experience of using it.

**Best for:** Products where seeing is believing, tactile/visual products, anything where mechanism of action matters.

**Choose when:**
- Product does something visually satisfying or surprising
- Competitors make similar claims but don't show the product working
- Audience is skeptical of marketing speak and wants proof

**Budget:** Ranges widely — DIY with phone possible for simple use cases.

**Trade-offs:** Less effective for products with delayed or invisible benefits (supplements, financial services).

---

### 3. Listicle
Half list, half article — "Three reasons to buy X." Familiar structure creates clear progression and anticipation.

**Key:** Headline is everything. Speak to your target demographic and mention a real problem they have.

**Best for:** Multiple features/benefits, audience education, products solving several pain points.

**Choose when:**
- You have 3–5 distinct selling points and don't know which will resonate
- Product is multifunctional or serves different use cases
- Audience is early in journey and needs to understand value breadth
- Want to test messaging angles within one ad

**Audience fit:** Best for unaware and problem-aware audiences who need education.

**Budget:** Low to mid.

---

### 4. Montage
Quick cuts using multiple shots — like a training montage. Lifestyle/aspirational vibes.

**Best for:** Lifestyle/aspirational brands, showing product in multiple contexts, building emotional resonance.

**Choose when:**
- Brand is the product (fashion, beauty, lifestyle)
- Goal is desire, not education
- You have strong visual assets already

**Audience fit:** Effective for product-aware and most-aware audiences who already know you but need emotional pull to act.

**Budget:** Cheap if you have existing content library. Scales with original production.

**Trade-offs:** Easy to make ones that look good but don't convert — need clear CTA or point of tension.

---

### 5. Split Screen
Divides screen into sections for simultaneous comparison — before/after, us vs. them, problem vs. solution.

**Best for:** Direct comparisons, before/after transformations, challenger brand positioning.

**Choose when:**
- Product has a clear, visual advantage over alternatives
- Before/after transformation is dramatic
- Comparison can be *shown*, not just claimed

**Audience fit:** Strong for solution-aware audiences comparing options.

**Budget:** Low to mid. Can be done with static images.

**Trade-offs:** Requires genuine visual difference — if not immediately obvious, format loses power.

## Three Underrated Formats Trending in 2026

*Theme: all three are good for building trust — a major priority given Meta's Andromeda update and increased audience skepticism.*

### 1. Behind the Scenes
Brings the buyer into your world — real brand, real people, real products. Builds trust, transparency, and authenticity.

**Best for:** Founder-led brands, craft/quality stories, humanizing corporate brands, transparency plays.

**Choose when:**
- Your process is a differentiator (handmade, locally sourced, unique method)
- Audience values authenticity and wants to know who's behind this
- Building trust in a category plagued by skepticism

**Audience fit:** Problem-aware and solution-aware audiences evaluating whether to trust you.

**Budget:** Very low. Authenticity wins — a founder on an iPhone often outperforms produced content. Over-production defeats the purpose.

**Trade-offs:** Requires someone willing to be on camera and a genuinely interesting story. Doesn't work for commodity products.

---

### 2. Tutorial / How-To
Product as a tool helping someone reach an outcome — outcome-focused, not product-focused. "Nobody wants a drill bit, they want a one-inch hole."

**Best for:** Products with a learning curve, multi-step routines, skill-building categories.

**Choose when:**
- Product requires or benefits from proper use
- Audience is searching for solutions (high intent)
- You can genuinely teach something valuable, not just pitch

**Audience fit:** Strongest for problem-aware audiences actively seeking solutions. Intercepts search intent and positions you as the answer.

**Budget:** Low to mid. Clarity matters more than production quality.

**Trade-offs:** If the tutorial feels like a thinly veiled pitch, it loses credibility.

---

### 3. Unboxing
Model or creator opening your product, ideally setting it up or beginning to use it. Helps viewers picture your product in their lives.

**Best for:** Premium products, subscription boxes, products where packaging is part of the experience, first-impression moments.

**Choose when:**
- Packaging and presentation are a selling point
- Audience cares about the "reveal" moment
- You want to reduce purchase anxiety by showing exactly what arrives
- Product category has elevated unboxing culture (tech, beauty, luxury)

**Audience fit:** Works across awareness levels — strongest for product-aware audiences on the edge of purchase.

**Budget:** Very low. Authentic phone footage often beats over-produced versions.

**Trade-offs:** If your packaging isn't impressive, an unboxing will expose that.

## Quick Decision Guide

| You want to... | Format |
|---|---|
| Build trust with skeptics | Testimonial or Behind the Scenes |
| Show the product working | Demo |
| Explain multiple benefits | Listicle |
| Create desire / lifestyle aspiration | Montage |
| Show before/after or vs. comparison | Split Screen |
| Show packaging as a selling point | Unboxing |
| Teach outcome-focused use | Tutorial / How-To |

**Formats often overlap** — a demo can be a montage, a montage can involve unboxing, etc.

---

## Format × Awareness Level Match

| Awareness Level | Best Formats |
|---|---|
| Unaware | Listicle, Behind the Scenes |
| Problem-aware | Tutorial, Testimonial, Split Screen |
| Solution-aware | Demo, Testimonial, Split Screen, Unboxing |
| Product-aware | Montage, Demo, Unboxing |
| Most-aware | Montage |
