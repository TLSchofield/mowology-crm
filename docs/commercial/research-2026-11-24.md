## CASL / privacy (summary of official text)
- Commercial electronic messages need express or implied consent; the sender carries the burden of proof (Act s.13).
- "Conspicuously published" exemption (s.10(9)(b)): address published by the person, no statement refusing unsolicited messages, AND the message is
  relevant to their business role. A law-firm summary reports CRTC's view that mere public availability is not enough (secondary source).
- Identification: sender business name (and on whose behalf), mailing address, plus phone, email or web address (Regulations s.2).
- Unsubscribe: required, free, honoured within 10 business days (s.6, s.11).
- Applies to messages to business addresses (no general B2B exemption found). Penalties up to $10M per violation for businesses.
- Express consent and other implied categories, and record-keeping periods: **unverified** — ask a lawyer.
- BC PIPA: "contact information" is not personal information; PIPEDA s.4.01 is narrower in practice (OPC).

**Three questions for a lawyer before first outreach:** (1) does a list built from published landscaping-owner website addresses qualify as conspicuous
publication and what does the relevance test need from my copy; (2) what proof-of-consent records, for how long, including address source; (3) is a sole
proprietor's business email personal information, and which regime applies.
